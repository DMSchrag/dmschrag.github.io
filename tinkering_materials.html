Mastering Tinkering

Properties of Tinkering Materials

What is useful and what is not

What are some good properties

For once when I started tinkering more it hit me like a truck that stuff costs money, especially if you want quality. I would really like to use Maxon DC Motors but they are just way more expensive than what I could get on Aliexpress.

The availability of Datasheets and Instructions are also needed if you go more into electronics hardware, I can't probably use a nice microcontroller if there is absolutely no documentation about it. It hasn't happened to me yet but I have already purchased some small knock off microcontrollers which were really difficult to set up since documentation was lacking.

My parts when I build something needs to be interoperable, when I tinkered on my commuter bike really wanting to mount that basket on my bike, there was no other choice then to design and build a mount on my own. I could have bought a nice bike basket but that wouldnt have fit with my expectations. Also a cheap basket and 3D printing a bit of plastic is quite cheaper than buying one off the shelve.

Sustainability, environmental impact and ethical properties should also be considered. A material can be cheap and easy to use but create a lot of waste or be difficult to repair. For electronics it is also difficult to know where all the metals, chips and plastic came from and under which conditions they were produced.

The beams are sturdy though and aluminium can be recycled when it is separated from the other parts. Aluminium mining can have bad environmental effects and bad working conditions. It is difficult to know where the aluminium came from.

Still feels a bit novel because you can turn normal objects, fruit, water or drawings into buttons. The plastic screws still need to be reused many times to make them worth it. Using waste cardboard is good because it is cheap and accessible. There is not much information about where the plastic screws come from or under which conditions they were produced.

Material selection observations

The materials are not all useful in the same situation. Arduino, Raspberry Pi and the AI and IOT Education Kit are mostly useful for electronics and programming. Lego, Meccano, MakerBeam and Makedo are more useful for building physical structures. Makey Makey is somewhere between physical materials and electronics because it can make normal conductive objects interactive.

MakerBeam is useful when something needs to be sturdy and still easy to take apart. Makey Makey is useful when I want to test an interaction quickly without making a full electronic circuit. Makedo cardboard screws are useful when I want to build something large and cheap before making a more permanent version.

Critical properties

At first I mostly considered price, documentation, interoperability and how easy a material is to start using. Sustainability is also important because tinkering creates a lot of prototypes which do not always work. A reusable material means that a failed project does not immediately become waste.

Environmental impact is different from only looking at whether something can be reused. Electronics need metals, circuit boards, plastic and energy to produce. Aluminium also needs a lot of energy, but can last for a long time and be recycled. Cardboard has a lower impact when old boxes are reused, but the plastic connectors still need to be used often.

Ethical properties are difficult to evaluate because it is not always clear where the materials came from. Metals can come from mining with bad effects on workers and local environments. Electronics can also collect personal data, especially when cameras, microphones, AI or internet connections are used. Good documentation should not only explain how a product works, but also where it came from, how it can be repaired and how it should be recycled.

AI & IOT Education Kit Is a set for 420 Euros, which makes it semi affordable. It is plug and play and can be easily used. At the moment the interface is in chinese and noone can read it. For getting into electronics it seems quite usable, there are a lot of sensors, which you can easily plug and play. Between itself it works easily. Not really novel. The modules can be reused for different projects, which is good. Since everything is already in its own casing it could also be hard to repair something when one module breaks. It uses a lot of electronics, plastic casings and batteries. This takes materials and energy to produce and can become electronic waste afterwards. It is difficult to know where all the sensors, chips and metals came from and under which working conditions they were made. The AI and IOT sensors can also collect data, so you should think about privacy.
Arduino Easily affordable. You need to watch a few videos to get into it. More than enough usability. Programming is free, and Arduino IDE comes with a lot of libraries pre installed. Can be connected to a lot of stuff through multiple ways. Not really novel. Can be reused for a lot of projects and for a long time if you do not solder everything permanently to it. It still is electronics, so producing the board, chips and metals uses energy and resources. When it breaks it becomes electronic waste. It is hard to see where every chip and metal comes from and under which conditions it was made. The open documentation is good because people are not fully dependent on one company.
Raspberry Pi More expensive up to 150 Euro for pi 5 with 8gb. You also need to watch a few videos to get into it. Lots of documentation for a huge range of application. Will also be used in the future. You need to know how to set it up and install more stuff, afterwards once can programm in C or Python or more. So it has a wider variety. Can also be connected to a lot of stuff but does not have an ADC converter. Not really novel. It can be used as a computer, server or controller for a lot of different projects. This means it can stay useful for quite a long time instead of only doing one thing. Producing the board, processor and memory takes metals, energy and water. It also needs electricity while it is running and becomes electronic waste when it breaks. The minerals and chips have long supply chains which are difficult to check. Projects with cameras, microphones or internet connections can also collect personal data.
Lego Has gotten expensive. It is the definition of plug and play. And the only thing hurting is when you cant get the bricks apart. Super nice for prototyping small scale stuff. You cant break lego, but one should go away from lego and consider other manufacturers. Also easy to get into super low threshhold. Works between itself and other manufacturers of building bricks of the same size. Not really novel. Lego lasts for a very long time and can be reused for hundreds of projects. It can also be bought second hand and passed on to other people. Lego is mostly plastic and needs oil and energy to be produced. It lasts long, but lost and broken bricks can stay in the environment for a very long time. It is expensive, which means not everyone has the same access to it. It is also important where the plastic comes from and under which conditions the bricks are produced.
Meccano It is alright. Easy enough lots of screws and plates to connect. Sturdy and cant be easily broken, parts could also be made on their own. It needs a bit of fine feeling to tinker with it when you want to scre something. Between itself it works well not really compatible with other stuff. Not really novel. The metal plates and screws are sturdy and can be taken apart and reused for many projects. Broken or missing parts can also sometimes be replaced on their own. Producing metal takes a lot of energy and mining. The parts last a long time and the metal can be recycled, which makes it better when it is used often. Mining metal can have bad effects on workers and local environments. It is difficult to see where the metal came from and under which conditions it was produced.
MakerBeam It is alright, but gets expensive if you need a lot of beams, screws and connection plates. Easy enough, you mostly screw beams and plates together. You can get into it without needing a lot of instructions. Sturdy and can be reused a lot. The small screws can easily get lost though. Quite low, but you need a bit of patience and a screwdriver to connect everything correctly. Between itself it works very well. Connecting motors, electronics or parts from other systems could need a custom made mount. Not really novel, it is basically a smaller building profile system. Can be taken apart and reused many times. You do not have to throw the complete construction away when one idea does not work. Aluminium takes a lot of energy to produce. The beams are sturdy though and aluminium can be recycled when it is separated from the other parts. Aluminium mining can have bad environmental effects and bad working conditions. It is difficult to know where the aluminium came from.
Makey Makey A bit expensive for what it does, but you get the board, cables and crocodile clips in one set. Very plug and play. After watching one or two examples you can get into it quite easily. Can be used over and over again. The cables and crocodile clips can break or get lost before the board does. Super low threshold. You connect conductive stuff and the computer sees it like a keyboard. Works with a lot of conductive materials and computers. It is not really made for every type of electronics project though. Still feels a bit novel because you can turn normal objects, fruit, water or drawings into buttons. Can be reused for a lot of projects and the crocodile clips mean you do not have to solder everything together permanently. It still is an electronic board with plastic, metal, cables and crocodile clips. When it breaks it can become electronic waste. The materials and electronic components are difficult to trace. It is not very clear where they came from or under which conditions they were made.
Makedo cardboard screws The screws are alright and cardboard can mostly be found for free. It is quite affordable for building bigger prototypes. Very easy. You make a hole and screw the cardboard parts together. It does not need a lot of instructions. The screws are sturdy and reusable. The cardboard is not precious but can easily be replaced when it breaks. Very low threshold and quite safe to start with. You do not need a lot of tools or experience. Works with most cardboard and can also be combined with tape, rope, wooden sticks and other craft materials. Not really novel, but the special screws make cardboard way more useful for prototyping. Very reusable because the screws can be used again and old cardboard gets another use before it is recycled. Reusing cardboard has quite a low impact compared to buying new building materials. The plastic screws still need to be reused many times to make them worth it. Using waste cardboard is good because it is cheap and accessible. There is not much information about where the plastic screws come from or under which conditions they were produced.
